## Design Context

### Users
Primary user: adult children, typically 25-45, who care about their parents' health but do not have medical training. They are usually on mobile, short on time, and want a faster, clearer way to interpret reports.

Secondary user: parents, typically 50-75, who receive the generated video explanation. The experience should stay legible, calm, and easy to understand on a phone without assuming medical literacy.

Core job to be done: turn dense checkup data into credible, emotionally warm, shareable guidance that lowers anxiety and reduces the effort of explaining health results by hand.

### Brand Personality
Caring, trustworthy, plainspoken.

The product should feel like a thoughtful family companion with medical seriousness, not a cold hospital portal and not a generic AI productivity dashboard.

Emotional goals: reassurance, warmth, calm confidence, and practical helpfulness.

### Aesthetic Direction
Light-first visual system. Favor soft neutral backgrounds, slate text, cyan or teal trust cues, and orange or rose accents for warmth, highlights, and upgrade moments.

Use rounded cards, generous spacing, subtle gradients, and restrained motion. Preserve a human, family-centered tone inspired by the existing banner and copy, but avoid anything childish, overly playful, or toy-like.

Typography should stay readable and bilingual-first: DM Sans and Noto Sans SC for body clarity, with Instrument Serif reserved for selective emphasis and warmth.

Avoid sterile enterprise-medical UI, dark cyber aesthetics, neon wellness palettes, or purple-heavy AI styling. Default assumption is light mode first unless the product direction changes.

Accessibility defaults: respect reduced motion, maintain at least WCAG AA contrast for core text and controls, keep touch targets comfortable, and prioritize copy clarity over visual cleverness.

### Design Principles
1. Translate before decorating. Every screen should reduce the cognitive burden of understanding health information.
2. Warmth must reinforce trust. Emotional design should make the product feel humane without weakening credibility.
3. Mobile clarity wins. Optimize for fast scanning, large tap targets, and straightforward sharing flows.
4. Calm hierarchy over dense dashboards. Use spacing, cards, and progressive disclosure to make medical content feel manageable.
5. Privacy should stay visible. Security, data handling, and result integrity should be easy to notice without dominating the interface.
